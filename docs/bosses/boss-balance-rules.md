# Boss Balance Rules

This document defines the balance rules for Old Town bosses. Bosses are calibrated to sit beside open-world combat, not replace it. The goal is to create memorable encounters that reward different playstyles without making any single boss mandatory for efficient progression.

## Effort Bands

Bosses are organised into four effort bands based on attention required and reward quality:

| Band | Attention | Combat Level | Reward Quality | Failure Cost | Examples |
|------|-----------|--------------|----------------|--------------|----------|
| **Starter** | Low | 1–10 | Modest drops, trophies | Lost food, retreat | The Cellar King, The Bell Bat Mother, Old Snapper |
| **First-Ring** | Medium | 20–30 | Good drops, rare uniques | Lost food, tool damage, death possible | Crowpost Jack, Aunt Bracken's Stump, The Cut Bell |
| **First-Ring Dangerous** | High | 25–35 | Rare drops, trophies, unique materials | Lost items, death likely | The Ferry Eel, Kiln-Worm Noll Saw, The Cut Bell |
| **Second-Ring** | High | 30–40+ | Rare drops, unique materials, cosmetics | Lost items, death likely | Crownheart Antler-King, Blackbar Foreman, Redcord Champion |

## Combat Level Calibration

### Starter Bosses

Starter bosses are designed for combat levels 1–10. They are the first named combat encounters a player fights. They must be forgiving and teach basic combat.

- **Health:** Low to medium. The player can defeat the boss in a short fight.
- **Damage:** Low. The player can survive without food, but food is recommended.
- **Mechanics:** One primary mechanic. The player learns one thing.
- **Failure:** Retreat is always possible. Death is rare.
- **Drops:** Common drops are modest. Rare drops are rare but not mandatory.

### First-Ring Bosses

First-ring bosses are designed for combat levels 20–30. They are repeatable area bosses that require preparation and attention.

- **Health:** Medium to high. The player must bring food and potions.
- **Damage:** Medium. The player must eat food during the fight.
- **Mechanics:** One primary mechanic and one secondary mechanic. The player learns two things.
- **Failure:** Retreat is possible. Death is possible but avoidable with preparation.
- **Drops:** Common drops are useful. Rare drops are valuable but not mandatory.

### First-Ring Dangerous Bosses

First-ring dangerous bosses are designed for combat levels 25–35. They are lair bosses with high risk and high reward.

- **Health:** High. The player must bring plenty of food and potions.
- **Damage:** High. The player must eat food frequently and use defensive abilities.
- **Mechanics:** One primary mechanic, one secondary mechanic, and one hazard. The player learns three things.
- **Failure:** Retreat is possible. Death is likely if the player is unprepared.
- **Drops:** Common drops are valuable. Rare drops are rare and exciting.

### Second-Ring Bosses

Second-ring bosses are designed for combat levels 30–40+. They are seeded for later expansion and are not fully documented.

- **Health:** Very high. The player must bring maximum supplies.
- **Damage:** Very high. The player must use all defensive abilities and food.
- **Mechanics:** Multiple mechanics. The player must master all mechanics.
- **Failure:** Retreat is possible. Death is likely if the player makes mistakes.
- **Drops:** Rare drops are the primary reward. Common drops are still useful.

## Reward Calibration

### Drop Calibration

Boss drops are calibrated to be exciting but not mandatory:

- **Starter bosses:** Common drops are basic materials. Rare drops are modest uniques.
- **First-ring bosses:** Common drops are useful materials. Rare drops are valuable uniques.
- **Dangerous bosses:** Common drops are valuable materials. Rare drops are rare uniques and trophies.
- **Second-ring bosses:** Common drops are high-tier materials. Rare drops are endgame uniques and cosmetics.

### Rare Drop Rates

Rare drops are calibrated to feel special without being obligatory:

- **Starter bosses:** Rare drops at 1 in 20 kills. The player earns the drop over time.
- **First-ring bosses:** Rare drops at 1 in 15 kills. The player farms the boss for the drop.
- **Dangerous bosses:** Rare drops at 1 in 10 kills. The player risks more for the drop.
- **Second-ring bosses:** Rare drops at 1 in 5 kills. The player masters the boss for the drop.

### Trophy Drop Rates

Trophies are calibrated to be proof items that players earn over time:

- **Starter bosses:** Trophies at 1 in 5 kills.
- **First-ring bosses:** Trophies at 1 in 5 kills.
- **Dangerous bosses:** Trophies at 1 in 3 kills.
- **Second-ring bosses:** Trophies at 1 in 3 kills.

## Failure Cost Rules

### Safe Bosses

Safe bosses have no failure cost beyond lost food:

- **The Cellar King:** Retreat to the Sootcellar. No death penalty.
- **The Bell Bat Mother:** Retreat to the Bellwood Copse. No death penalty.
- **Old Snapper:** Retreat to the River Stoop. No death penalty.

### Risky Bosses

Risky bosses have failure costs that include lost food and tool damage:

- **Mudhook Grib:** Retreat to the North Quarry Road. Death is possible but rare.
- **Crowpost Jack:** Retreat to the Crowmile Road. Death is possible.
- **Aunt Bracken's Stump:** Retreat to the Bellwood Copse. No death penalty.
- **Hidepeg Horror:** Retreat to the Patchfield. Death is possible.
- **The Wrong Flower:** Retreat to the Lowgrave Chapel. Death is possible.
- **The Door That Bites:** Retreat to the Sootstairs. Death is possible.
- **Gravekeeper's Wrong Son:** Retreat to the Lowgrave Chapel. Death is possible.

### Dangerous Bosses

Dangerous bosses have failure costs that include death and significant item loss:

- **The Cut Bell:** Retreat to the Tinstone Cut surface. Death is possible. Lost tools.
- **The Ferry Eel:** Retreat to the Wardenbrook dock. Death is possible. Lost bait.
- **Kiln-Worm Noll Saw:** Retreat to the Old Kiln surface. Death is possible. Lost beads.

### Death Rules

- **Safe bosses:** No death possible. Retreat is the only failure.
- **Risky bosses:** Death is possible but rare. The player is more likely to retreat.
- **Dangerous bosses:** Death is possible and likely if unprepared. The player must bring food and potions.
- **Death penalty:** Standard Old Town death penalty applies (loss of carried items, respawn at nearest safe point). No special boss death penalty.

## Anti-Power-Creep Rules

1. **No boss should be the best at everything.** A boss can be best for fun, best for cosmetics, or best for a specific drop — but not best for all drops.
2. **Open-world combat must remain viable.** If a boss is the only way to train a combat skill, the open-world method must be buffed or the boss must be nerfed.
3. **Boss drops must avoid power creep.** No combat gear, no global buffs, no best-in-slot items. Cosmetics and narrow utility only.
4. **New bosses must not obsolete old ones.** A new boss can be better for a specific niche, but it cannot make an existing boss irrelevant.
5. **Rare drops must not be mandatory.** The Tallow Drake Scale, Argent Ray clue, and Gravekeeper's Tooth fragment are cool but not required for progression.

## Balance Testing

Before a boss is released, it must be tested against the following questions:

1. **Is it fun without rewards?** A player should enjoy the fight even if they earn no drops.
2. **Is it optional?** A player who ignores the boss should not feel behind in progression.
3. **Is it fair?** The failure cost should match the reward. Dangerous bosses should not feel like cheap death traps.
4. **Is it social?** Bosses should not punish solo players or force grouping.
5. **Is it sustainable?** The boss should not deplete resources or create inflation.
