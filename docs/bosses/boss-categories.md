# Boss Categories

This document defines the eight boss categories used in Old Town. Every boss belongs to exactly one primary category. The category determines the encounter structure, the risk level, the reward identity, and the player expectation.

## Category Definitions

| Category | Definition | Encounter Structure | Risk Level | Reward Identity |
|----------|------------|---------------------|------------|-----------------|
| **Starter Boss** | First named combat encounter. Low punishment. Teaches basic combat. | Simple mechanics, one or two attack patterns. | Safe. | Modest unique drops, trophies. |
| **Area Boss** | Repeatable boss tied to a specific area or district. | Full encounter with mechanics, lair rules, and unique drops. | Risky or dangerous. | Unique drops, materials, trophies. |
| **Warden Boss** | Assigned or boosted by Wardenry task. | Same as the base boss, but with Wardenry contract drops. | Depends on base boss. | Contract drops, proof items, boosted rewards. |
| **Skilling Boss** | Boss with a strong skilling connection. Skill actions matter. | Combat plus skill actions (candles, bait, beads, etc.). | Risky. | Skill materials, unique drops, crafting inputs. |
| **Quest Boss** | One-time or quest-unlocked repeatable encounter. | Encounter tied to a quest narrative. | Depends on quest. | Quest items, unique drops, narrative rewards. |
| **Lair Boss** | Boss with a dedicated room or arena and specific access rules. | Full encounter with lair mechanics, safe tiles, and hazards. | Dangerous. | Rare drops, trophies, lair-specific rewards. |
| **Roaming Threat** | Rare area danger. Not a true farm target. | Appears unpredictably. Short encounter. | Dangerous. | Rare drops, proof items. |
| **Duel Boss** | Single-combat skill-check enemy. Tests one combat style. | One-on-one encounter with a specific style weakness. | Risky. | Style-specific rewards, trophies, skill proof. |

## Category Rules

### Starter Boss

- Must be the first named combat encounter a player fights.
- Must have low punishment. Death is rare; retreat is more common.
- Must teach one basic combat mechanic (food timing, target swapping, movement).
- Must have a modest unique drop. Not a best-in-slot item.
- Must be accessible without a quest or complex unlock.
- Must not require a group. Solo players must be able to complete the encounter.
- Examples: The Cellar King, The Bell Bat Mother, Old Snapper.

### Area Boss

- Must be tied to a specific area or district. The area gives the boss its identity.
- Must be repeatable. Players can fight the boss as many times as they want.
- Must have a unique drop or material. The drop is narrow, not universally best.
- Must have a lair or specific spawn location. The boss is not a random wandering creature.
- Must not be a daily lock or time-limited event.
- Must not require a group. Solo players must be able to complete the encounter.
- Examples: Crowpost Jack, Aunt Bracken's Stump, The Cut Bell.

### Warden Boss

- Must be a boss that Wardenry can assign as a contract target.
- Must have the same base encounter as the non-Warden version. The Wardenry version adds drops, not mechanics.
- Must have contract-specific drops. These drops only appear when the boss is assigned by Wardenry.
- Must not require Wardenry to fight. The boss is accessible without a contract.
- Must not have a group requirement. Solo players can complete the encounter.
- Examples: Mudhook Grib (Warden contract), The Cellar King (Warden contract), The Wrong Flower (Warden contract).

### Skilling Boss

- Must have a strong skilling connection. Skill actions (candles, bait, beads, etc.) matter in the fight.
- Must not be a pure skilling activity. The primary loop is combat. The skilling connection is secondary.
- Must have a skill item that helps. A specific tool, consumable, or spell makes the fight easier.
- Must not require the skill to fight. The skill helps but is not mandatory.
- Must have drops that connect to the skill. Materials, crafting inputs, or skill tools.
- Examples: Ashling in the Kiln (Tide beads reduce heat), The Wrong Flower (candles and Favour), Old Snapper (bait and fishing).

### Quest Boss

- Must be tied to a quest narrative. The quest introduces the boss or explains its existence.
- Must be one-time or repeatable depending on the quest. Some quest bosses become repeatable after the quest.
- Must have quest-specific drops. Items that are needed for the quest or its follow-up.
- Must not require the quest to fight after the first time. The boss becomes accessible without the quest.
- Must not be a mandatory progression gate. The player can skip the quest and still fight the boss later.
- Examples: The Wrong Flower (Gravegate Flowers), Ashling in the Kiln (Smoke Over Old Town), The Cut Bell (Mud on the North Road).

### Lair Boss

- Must have a dedicated room or arena. The lair has specific rules, safe tiles, and hazards.
- Must have a specific access route. The player must travel to the lair, not just walk up to the boss.
- Must have lair mechanics. The room itself is part of the fight (safe tiles, hazard pulses, resource objects).
- Must have a visible threshold. The player knows they are entering the lair before the fight starts.
- Must not wander into safe hubs. The boss stays in the lair.
- Examples: The Cut Bell (Tinstone Cut), The Door That Bites (Sootstairs), Kiln-Worm Noll Saw (Old Kiln).

### Roaming Threat

- Must be a rare area danger. It appears unpredictably and does not have a fixed lair.
- Must not be a farm target. The threat is too rare or too dangerous to be a reliable source of drops.
- Must have a short encounter. The fight is brief, not a sustained boss encounter.
- Must have a rare drop or proof item. The reward is for surviving the encounter, not for farming.
- Must not be a daily lock or time-limited event.
- Examples: Grave Wisp Swarm (Lowgrave), Bell Boar Stampede (Bellwood Copse), River Snapper Pack (Wardenbrook).

### Duel Boss

- Must be a single-combat skill-check enemy. The boss tests one combat style or mechanic.
- Must have a specific style weakness. A specific weapon, spell, or tactic makes the fight easier.
- Must be one-on-one. The boss does not summon adds or create hazards.
- Must have a style-specific reward. The reward is tied to the combat style the boss teaches.
- Must not require a group. The duel is solo by definition.
- Examples: Redcord Champion (parry/riposte), Blackbar Foreman (shield break), Argent Penitent (cleanse/support).

## Category Overlap

A boss can have secondary elements from other categories, but its primary category is the one that answers the question "what is the player fighting most of the time?"

- **The Cellar King** is primarily a Starter Boss (first encounter) with Warden Boss elements (Wardenry contract). Primary category: Starter Boss.
- **Mudhook Grib** is primarily an Area Boss (North Quarry Road) with Warden Boss elements (Wardenry contract). Primary category: Area Boss.
- **Ashling in the Kiln** is primarily a Skilling Boss (Tide beads, heat mechanic) with Lair Boss elements (Old Kiln room). Primary category: Skilling Boss.
- **The Wrong Flower** is primarily a Quest Boss (Gravegate Flowers) with Skilling Boss elements (candles, Favour). Primary category: Quest Boss.
- **The Cut Bell** is primarily a Lair Boss (Tinstone Cut arena) with Area Boss elements (Tinstone Cut area). Primary category: Lair Boss.
- **Crowpost Jack** is primarily an Area Boss (Crowmile Road) with Warden Boss elements (Wardenry contract). Primary category: Area Boss.
- **Redcord Champion** is primarily a Duel Boss (parry test) with Quest Boss elements (Carmine Yard quest). Primary category: Duel Boss.

## Banned Category Combinations

Some category combinations are banned because they create design problems:

| Combination | Why it is banned | What to use instead |
|-------------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Starter Boss + Lair Boss** | A first encounter with a complex lair is overwhelming. | Make the starter boss simple. Add a lair later for the first-ring version. |
| **Roaming Threat + Area Boss** | A roaming threat is too rare to be a farm target. An area boss is too reliable to be a roaming threat. | Pick one. If the boss is a farm target, make it an area boss. If it is a rare danger, make it a roaming threat. |
| **Duel Boss + Skilling Boss** | A duel is pure combat. A skilling boss requires skill actions. The loops are incompatible. | Make the boss one or the other. If it needs skill actions, it is a skilling boss. |
| **Warden Boss + Lair Boss** | A Warden boss should be accessible without a complex lair. The contract adds drops, not mechanics. | Make the Warden boss an area boss or a skilling boss. The lair is optional. |
| **Quest Boss + Roaming Threat** | A quest boss is tied to a narrative. A roaming threat is unpredictable. The player cannot find a quest boss if it roams. | Make the quest boss a fixed lair or area boss. |
